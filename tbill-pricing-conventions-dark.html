T-Bill Pricing: Conventions Cheat-Sheet

Notation, formulas, and the resolved points of confusion from our conversation.

Notation

$P_τ$
Price of \$1, to be paid in $τ$ years (aka the discount factor)
$τ$
Year-fraction to maturity; day-count specified per convention below
$d_τ$
"Bank discount rate", the Bill's own auction/secondary-market quote
$R_τ$
Investment Rate = Coupon-Equivalent Yield = Bond-Equivalent Yield = Investment Yield (all one quantity, per Treasury's own naming)
$Z_τ$
Annually-compounded zero rate
$Z_τ^{(2)}$
Semiannually-compounded zero rate

The conventions, side by side

Convention Defining equation for $P_τ$ Day count Base
Bank discount $P = 1 - d\,τ_{360}$ Act/360 face ($1)
Investment Rate
($τ \le \tfrac12$ yr)		 $P = (1 + Rτ)^{-1}$ Act/365 or 366 price (P)
Investment Rate
($τ > \tfrac12$ yr)		 $P = \left[(1+(τ-\tfrac12)R)(1+\tfrac{R}{2})\right]^{-1}$ Act/365 or 366 price (P)
Annual compounding $P = (1 + Z)^{-τ}$ (your choice) price (P)
Semiannual compounding $P = (1 + Z^{(2)}/2)^{-2τ}$ (your choice) price (P)

The two Investment-Rate rows are continuous at $τ = \tfrac12$ — both reduce to $P(1+R/2)=1$ exactly at the seam, so there's no ambiguity right at 6 months.

Worked examples we ran

13-week bill, P = 0.85
d ≈ 59.34%  |  R ≈ 70.78%  |  Z ≈ 91.91%  |  $Z^{(2)}$ ≈ 77.06%
90-day bill, P = 0.997
d = 1.200%  |  R ≈ 1.2203%  (bridge formula confirmed match)
Published $R_{90}$ = 4.3% (90-day CMT point)
$P = (1+0.043\times 90/365)^{-1} \approx 0.989505$  →  Price ≈ $98.9505 / $100
Published $R_{182}$ = 4.3% (6-month CMT point)
$τ = \tfrac12$ exactly → $P = (1+0.043/2)^{-1} \approx 0.978953$  →  Price ≈ $97.8953 / $100. It's a bill (26-week), not a bond.

Key confusions we resolved

  • $d_τ$ and $R_τ$ are different rates f or the same $P_τ$ — not two formulas competing for the same quantity. Discount rate is off face, act/360; investment rate is off price, act/365.
  • $R_τ$ itself has two regimes ($τ \le \tfrac12$ vs $τ > \tfrac12$ year), because it mirrors how a real semiannual bond accrues: compound only at whole coupon dates, simple-interest any partial stub. Under ½ year, there's no whole period to compound — hence pure simple interest.
  • The published CMT / Daily Par Yield Curve number, even at bill maturities, is $R_τ$ (not $d_τ$, not $Z_τ^{(2)}$) — Treasury explicitly converts bill discount rates to bond-equivalent yields before using them as curve inputs.
  • "Yield" is overloaded: a "CMT yield" is a nominal annual rate under semiannual-compounding convention, reported "on a simple annualized basis" — not an effective/compounded return. Converting it to a true APY requires $(1+I/2)^2 - 1$.
